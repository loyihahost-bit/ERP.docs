# Notification Domain

**Document ID:** DA-14
**Status:** Accepted
**Version:** 1.0
**Scope:** FastFood ERP
**Parent Document:** `docs/03_Domain_Analysis/01_Domain_Overview.md`

## 1. Purpose

The Notification domain manages application-level notifications and operational alerts generated by important business and system conditions.

It is responsible for:

* notification creation;
* notification severity;
* recipient resolution;
* Business and Branch scope;
* unread/read state;
* resolved/expired state;
* deduplication;
* notification history;
* notification actions and deep links;
* retry and delivery state;
* offline notification handling;
* notification configuration boundaries.

The domain does not own the business condition that caused the notification.

---

# 2. Notification Principle

Notifications communicate events or conditions.

They do not become the source of truth for the underlying business state.

The logical relationship is:

```text id="m8q3v6"
Business Condition / Event
        ↓
Notification
        ↓
Recipient Resolution
        ↓
Delivery
        ↓
Read / Resolved / Expired
```

---

# 3. Notification Types

Examples include:

* Low Stock;
* Out of Stock;
* Subscription Ending;
* Salary Due;
* Large Refund;
* Large Inventory Variance;
* Branch Loss;
* Printer Failure;
* Important Operational Failure.

The list may evolve as new business requirements are introduced.

---

# 4. Notification Identity

Each logical notification has a stable Notification UUID.

Duplicate processing of the same underlying condition must not create duplicate active notifications.

---

# 5. Severity

Notifications support:

* `Info`;
* `Warning`;
* `Critical`.

Severity determines presentation priority.

Severity does not itself grant the recipient any additional permission.

---

# 6. Info

Info notifications communicate useful operational information.

They generally do not require immediate intervention.

Examples may include:

* report completed;
* payroll calculation completed;
* non-critical configuration information.

---

# 7. Warning

Warning notifications indicate a condition that may require attention.

Examples:

* low stock;
* subscription approaching expiry;
* pending operational issue.

---

# 8. Critical

Critical notifications indicate an important condition requiring prompt attention.

Examples may include:

* severe inventory variance;
* significant operational failure;
* important security or system condition.

Critical notification presentation may be prominent.

It must not unnecessarily block normal POS operation.

---

# 9. Recipient Resolution

Recipients are determined by predefined event scope.

Possible recipients include:

* Owner;
* Manager;
* authorized employee.

A notification must not be sent to every employee by default.

---

# 10. Owner Notifications

Owner receives notifications for business conditions defined as Owner-relevant.

Examples include:

* significant refund;
* major inventory variance;
* branch loss;
* subscription lifecycle;
* important cash discrepancy.

The exact event-to-recipient mapping is configuration/business-rule driven.

---

# 11. Manager Notifications

Managers receive notifications only when:

* the notification type allows Manager recipients;
* the Manager has the relevant Business/Branch scope;
* required permission is available.

Being a Manager alone does not guarantee access to every notification.

---

# 12. Employee Notifications

An employee may receive a notification only when the event definition permits employee recipients and the employee has the required scope/permission.

Sensitive notifications must not be exposed to unauthorized employees.

---

# 13. Branch Scope

A Branch-scoped notification belongs to a specific Branch.

Only authorized recipients with access to that Branch may see it.

Example:

```text id="n4m7q2"
Branch 1
  ↓
Low Stock Alert
  ↓
Authorized Branch 1 recipients
```

Branch 2 users must not receive Branch 1 notifications without explicit cross-Branch authority.

---

# 14. Business Scope

Business-level notifications belong to the Business.

They may be visible to authorized Business-level recipients.

Business-level notification access must still respect permission and subscription rules.

---

# 15. Tenant Isolation

Notifications are strictly tenant-isolated.

A notification belonging to Business A must never become visible to Business B.

Isolation applies to:

* creation;
* storage;
* queries;
* background jobs;
* offline synchronization;
* actions;
* history.

---

# 16. Active Notification

A notification remains active while its underlying condition is unresolved and the notification has not otherwise reached its terminal state.

Repeated evaluation of the same active condition must not create unlimited duplicate notifications.

---

# 17. Deduplication

The system maintains logical deduplication for the same condition.

Conceptually:

```text id="c6v2p8"
Condition Active
     ↓
Notification A

Condition still Active
     ↓
No duplicate Notification A
```

---

# 18. Condition Resolution

When the underlying condition is resolved, the notification may transition to `Resolved`.

The notification remains in history.

It is not silently deleted.

---

# 19. Recurring Conditions

If a condition resolves and later occurs again, it starts a new notification cycle.

Example:

```text id="r7k3m9"
Low Stock
  ↓
Active
  ↓
Resolved
  ↓
Stock becomes low again
  ↓
New notification cycle
```

This prevents unrelated occurrences from being merged into one endless notification.

---

# 20. Read State

Notification read state is recipient-specific.

One recipient reading a notification must not automatically mark it read for every other recipient.

---

# 21. Unread State

A newly delivered notification is normally `Unread`.

The recipient may mark it as `Read`.

Read state is separate from the underlying condition.

A notification may be:

```text
Read + Active
```

or:

```text
Unread + Resolved
```

---

# 22. Notification Resolution vs Read

`Read` and `Resolved` represent different concepts.

* **Read** = recipient has viewed the notification.
* **Resolved** = underlying condition is no longer active.

Reading a notification does not resolve the underlying business problem.

---

# 23. Expired Notification

Some notifications may become irrelevant after a defined deadline.

Such notifications may transition to `Expired`.

Expiration is distinct from Read and Resolved.

---

# 24. Notification History

Notification history preserves:

* Notification UUID;
* type;
* severity;
* Business;
* Branch where applicable;
* recipients;
* creation time;
* state changes;
* resolution/expiration;
* related entity;
* action reference where applicable.

---

# 25. Related Entity

A notification may reference the entity responsible for the condition.

Examples:

* Product;
* Order;
* Payment;
* Cash Session;
* Inventory adjustment;
* Subscription;
* Payroll period.

The reference must not grant access by itself.

---

# 26. Deep Link

A notification may provide an action/deep link to the relevant application screen.

Before opening the target, the system must validate:

* authentication;
* Business scope;
* Branch scope;
* permission;
* subscription/lifecycle state.

A notification must never act as an authorization token.

---

# 27. Notification Actions

A notification action may allow a user to navigate to the relevant operational context.

Examples:

```text id="u5n8q4"
Low Stock
   ↓
Open Inventory

Subscription Ending
   ↓
Open Subscription

Cash Difference
   ↓
Open Cash Session
```

The Notification domain does not automatically perform business corrections.

---

# 28. Automatic Correction Prohibition

Notification processing must not automatically:

* change inventory;
* change payments;
* modify Orders;
* alter cash;
* modify payroll;
* change permissions.

Notifications communicate conditions.

Business actions remain controlled by their owning domains.

---

# 29. Critical Presentation

Critical notifications may use prominent application-level presentation.

However, a Critical notification must not unnecessarily block:

* Order creation;
* Order acceptance;
* Payment;
* Cash Session operations;
* Inventory operations.

Operational POS continuity remains a system priority.

---

# 30. Application Channel

The current notification channel is the application itself.

This includes:

* notification center;
* banners;
* alerts;
* relevant UI indicators.

External channels are outside the current scope.

---

# 31. Future Channels

Future notification channels may include:

* Email;
* SMS;
* Telegram;
* Mobile push;
* Other integrations.

Adding future channels must not change the underlying Notification identity and lifecycle model.

---

# 32. Delivery State

Application delivery may conceptually use:

```text id="e3q7m5"
Pending
   ↓
Delivered
```

Failure may produce:

```text id="x8m2v4"
Failed
   ↓
Retrying
   ↓
Delivered
```

The exact technical delivery mechanism belongs to the Architecture phase.

---

# 33. Delivery Failure

A notification delivery failure must not roll back the business transaction that caused the notification.

Example:

```text id="h4n9p2"
Inventory Adjustment
      ↓
Transaction Successful
      ↓
Notification Delivery Failed
```

The inventory operation remains successful.

The notification may be retried.

---

# 34. Retry

Notification delivery retries must be:

* bounded;
* idempotent;
* observable;
* isolated from the core transaction.

Repeated retries must not create duplicate logical notifications.

---

# 35. Retry Limit

After the configured retry limit is reached:

* the delivery operation may become `Failed`;
* the failure remains observable;
* the system may record the failure;
* the underlying business operation remains unchanged.

---

# 36. Offline Notifications

A trusted offline device may receive or generate supported local notifications.

Offline notifications must remain subject to:

* valid offline authorization;
* Employee status;
* Branch scope;
* permission;
* subscription constraints.

---

# 37. Offline Notification Reconciliation

Offline notification state may later synchronize with the server.

Synchronization must preserve:

* Notification UUID;
* underlying event relationship;
* recipient identity;
* state transitions;
* timestamps where applicable.

Duplicate synchronization must not create duplicate notifications.

---

# 38. Offline Condition Evaluation

The device may evaluate conditions based on locally available authoritative transaction state.

However, server state becomes authoritative after synchronization.

The device must not permanently override a server-resolved condition.

---

# 39. Subscription Notifications

Subscription lifecycle notifications may include:

* upcoming expiry;
* expiry;
* read-only transition;
* approaching deletion eligibility;
* deletion warning.

Notification timing follows Subscription domain rules.

---

# 40. Subscription Deduplication

The same subscription lifecycle threshold must not create unlimited duplicate notifications.

A new notification cycle may occur after the relevant lifecycle condition changes and later reoccurs.

---

# 41. Notification Configuration

Business-level configuration may control allowed thresholds for selected notification types.

Examples:

* large refund threshold;
* large inventory variance threshold;
* other supported business alert thresholds.

Configuration changes must preserve:

* previous value;
* new value;
* actor;
* timestamp;
* reason where required.

---

# 42. Threshold Configuration Boundary

The Notification domain consumes threshold configuration.

It does not modify the underlying transaction data merely because a threshold is crossed.

---

# 43. Notification and Audit

Important notification operations may be audited.

Examples include:

* notification configuration changes;
* threshold changes;
* important notification state transitions;
* administrative actions.

Routine notification reads do not necessarily require individual audit events.

---

# 44. Notification and Reports

Reports may include notification-related operational metrics.

The Notification domain remains authoritative for notification state and history.

Reports must not modify notifications.

---

# 45. Notification and Subscription

Subscription state may determine:

* whether notifications are generated;
* whether the Business may access notification history;
* which functions remain available.

Subscription remains authoritative for entitlement.

---

# 46. Notification and Employee

Employee state affects recipient eligibility.

An inactive Employee must not continue receiving new normal operational notifications after the applicable deactivation point.

Historical notifications remain preserved according to lifecycle rules.

---

# 47. Notification and Branch

Branch assignment affects recipient eligibility for Branch-scoped notifications.

A user switching Branches must not automatically gain access to historical notifications from the new Branch unless authorized.

---

# 48. Notification and Security

Notifications may contain sensitive information.

Therefore the notification payload should contain only the information required for the recipient's role and context.

Sensitive details should not be exposed to unauthorized users through:

* notification lists;
* previews;
* deep links;
* offline storage.

---

# 49. Notification and Audit History

Notification history should be reconstructable.

The system should be able to determine:

* why the notification was created;
* who received it;
* when it was read;
* whether the condition was resolved;
* whether delivery failed;
* whether an administrative action occurred.

---

# 50. Notification and Background Jobs

Background jobs may process:

* scheduled notification checks;
* notification delivery;
* retry;
* subscription threshold checks;
* cleanup according to lifecycle policy.

Background jobs must remain idempotent.

---

# 51. Notification and Error Handling

Notification failure is a secondary failure.

A notification failure must not convert a successful core business operation into a failed business transaction.

The Error Handling domain defines the global error behavior.

---

# 52. Concurrency

The Notification domain must handle concurrent:

* condition evaluations;
* notification creation;
* read operations;
* resolution;
* delivery retries;
* synchronization.

Duplicate active notifications must be prevented through deterministic identity/idempotency rules.

---

# 53. Notification Resolution Concurrency

If multiple workers detect the same resolved condition concurrently, they must not create conflicting notification states.

The final state must be deterministic.

---

# 54. Recipient Read Concurrency

If the same recipient marks a notification as read from multiple devices:

* the operation must be idempotent;
* no duplicate read records should be created.

---

# 55. Multi-Device Behavior

A logical notification belongs to the recipient, not to a specific device.

A recipient using multiple trusted devices should see the same logical notification state after synchronization.

Reading it on one device may synchronize the read state to another authorized device.

---

# 56. Notification Domain Services

Potential domain services include:

```text id="v2m7q5"
Notification Creation Service
Recipient Resolution Service
Notification Deduplication Service
Notification Delivery Service
Notification Retry Service
Condition Resolution Service
Notification Configuration Service
Notification Access Service
```

These are logical domain services and do not necessarily represent separate applications.

---

# 57. Domain Events

Potential events include:

```text id="n8q3f6"
NotificationCreated
NotificationDelivered
NotificationDeliveryFailed
NotificationRead
NotificationResolved
NotificationExpired
NotificationThresholdChanged
NotificationCycleStarted
```

Events represent facts that have already occurred.

---

# 58. Aggregate Boundaries

The Notification domain may conceptually contain:

* Notification;
* Notification Recipient;
* Notification State;
* Notification Delivery;
* Notification Action;
* Notification Configuration.

It does not own:

* Orders;
* Payments;
* Inventory;
* Cash Sessions;
* Employees;
* Payroll;
* Subscription;
* Audit;
* Reports.

---

# 59. Invariants

### Identity

1. Every logical notification has a stable UUID.
2. Duplicate processing of the same condition does not create duplicate active notifications.
3. Notification identity is independent from delivery attempt identity.
4. Notification history remains identifiable.

### Severity

5. Supported severities are Info, Warning, and Critical.
6. Severity does not grant permission.
7. Critical notifications do not automatically block POS operations.

### Scope

8. Every notification belongs to a Business.
9. Branch-scoped notifications identify their Branch.
10. Cross-Business notification access is prohibited.
11. Cross-Branch access requires authorization.
12. Notification queries enforce Business scope.
13. Notification queries enforce Branch scope.

### Recipients

14. Recipients are resolved according to notification rules.
15. Owner notifications are not automatically visible to every employee.
16. Manager notifications require appropriate scope and permission.
17. Employee notifications require appropriate authorization.
18. Inactive Employees do not receive new normal notifications after their applicable deactivation point.

### State

19. Read state is recipient-specific.
20. Read does not mean Resolved.
21. Resolved means the underlying condition has ended.
22. Expired is distinct from Read and Resolved.
23. Historical notification states remain reconstructable.

### Deduplication

24. The same active condition does not create unlimited duplicate notifications.
25. Condition resolution ends the current notification cycle.
26. A recurring condition may create a new notification cycle.
27. Retry does not create a new logical notification.

### Actions

28. Notification actions do not automatically grant authorization.
29. Deep links require normal access validation.
30. Notification actions cannot bypass permissions.
31. Notification actions cannot bypass subscription restrictions.
32. Notification actions cannot silently modify source business data.

### Delivery

33. Delivery failure does not roll back the core transaction.
34. Delivery retries are bounded.
35. Delivery retries are idempotent.
36. Failed delivery remains observable.
37. Delivery state is separate from business condition state.

### Offline

38. Offline notifications require valid trusted-device authorization.
39. Offline notifications preserve stable identity.
40. Offline synchronization is idempotent.
41. Server state remains authoritative after synchronization.
42. Offline notifications cannot bypass Branch scope.
43. Offline notifications cannot bypass permission.
44. Offline notifications cannot bypass subscription restrictions.

### Subscription

45. Subscription lifecycle notifications follow Subscription rules.
46. Subscription threshold notifications are deduplicated.
47. Subscription changes do not corrupt historical notification records.

### Configuration

48. Supported notification thresholds are configurable where permitted.
49. Threshold changes preserve previous values.
50. Threshold changes preserve new values.
51. Threshold changes preserve actor and time.
52. Notification configuration does not modify source business transactions.

### Security

53. Notification content respects recipient authorization.
54. Sensitive information is not exposed through unauthorized notification previews.
55. Offline notification storage respects device security.
56. Notification deep links require authorization checks.

### History

57. Notification creation time is preserved.
58. Recipient history is preserved.
59. Read state history is reconstructable where required.
60. Resolution history is preserved.
61. Delivery failure history is preserved where required.
62. Administrative notification configuration history is preserved.

### Concurrency

63. Concurrent condition evaluation does not create duplicate active notifications.
64. Concurrent resolution is deterministic.
65. Concurrent read operations are idempotent.
66. Multi-device notification state converges correctly.

### Integration

67. Notification failure does not roll back Order transactions.
68. Notification failure does not roll back Payment transactions.
69. Notification failure does not roll back Inventory transactions.
70. Notification failure does not roll back Cash transactions.
71. Reports consume notification information without modifying it.
72. Audit records important notification configuration/actions.
73. Subscription remains authoritative for entitlement.
74. Employee remains authoritative for employee status.
75. Branch remains authoritative for Branch scope.

---

# 60. Completion Criteria

The Notification domain is complete when:

* notification identity is defined;
* severity is defined;
* recipient resolution is defined;
* Business and Branch scope is defined;
* read state is defined;
* resolved state is defined;
* expired state is defined;
* deduplication is defined;
* recurring notification cycles are defined;
* deep-link boundaries are defined;
* delivery and retry are defined;
* critical presentation is defined;
* offline behavior is defined;
* synchronization behavior is defined;
* threshold configuration is defined;
* subscription notifications are defined;
* security boundaries are defined;
* history is reconstructable;
* concurrency rules are defined;
* aggregate boundaries are clear.
